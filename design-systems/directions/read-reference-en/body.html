Chapter one / concepts

How a schedule is built

This page explains the three rules behind route scheduling. Routes, platforms and times are demo data and do not describe real operations.

01

Shifts and capacity

How many shifts a route can run in a window follows from the number of platforms and the minimum headway. Changing either shifts the downstream connections, so confirm capacity before discussing the timetable.

headway = platforms × average dwell + buffer

The buffer is not slack: it is the room a delay needs to be absorbed. Below two minutes, a single servicing task pushes the whole route into delay.

02

When to add a shift

When a delay passes the threshold and the next three shifts are already full, adding a shift is safer than compressing the headway. The trade-offs:

ActionFits whenCost
Compress headwayDelay under 5 minutesDwell shrinks, the next shift is more likely to run late
Add a shiftNext three shifts are fullNeeds a free platform and driver
RebookOne shift affectedMoves cost to passengers; last resort
Why rebooking comes last

Rebooking transfers the cost to passengers; use it only when adding and compressing are both ruled out. Demo note, not a service promise.
